Baseline assumptions
  • Two armed organizations; no ungoverned vacuum when one leaves.
  • Actors maximize territorial control; initial control and military allocation are exogenous.
  • Civilians are boundedly rational, survival-sensitive, and perceive current control as stable.
  • Denunciations are local, visible to the victim’s family, and sent only to the villager’s associated organization.
  • Motives may be political or private; x and y are constant in the baseline.
  • u > i and y > x; violence production costs decrease with control.
  • The model predicts location, selectivity, and likely perpetrator—not timing or intensity.
Implementation assumptions
  • The logistic shape and steepness of q(r) are visualization choices.
  • Continuous interpolation between zones is illustrative.
  • Numeric payoffs use a symmetry-consistent reconstruction of p. 201.
  • The t₀→t₁ update is a teaching extension, not Kalyvas’s equation.
  • No output is an empirical probability, forecast, or causal estimate.
